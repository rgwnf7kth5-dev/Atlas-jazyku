The '''Proto-Elamite script''' is a [[writing system]] used briefly during the early [[Bronze Age]] before the introduction of [[Elamite cuneiform]]. It remains largely [[Undeciphered writing systems|undeciphered]], except for various numbers.<ref>Monroe, M. Willis, et al., "Recent Progress in Deciphering Proto-Elamite", [[Near Eastern Archaeology (journal)|Near Eastern Archaeology]] 88.4, pp. 314-323, 2025</ref>

There are many similarities between the writing systems of the Proto-Elamite tablets and the contemporaneous [[proto-cuneiform]] tablets of the [[Uruk period|Uruk IV period]] in Mesopotamia. Both are relatively isolated. Singletons aside, tablets have been found at only five Proto-Elamite sites. For comparison, Proto-cuneiform tablets have only been found at [[Uruk]], [[Jemdet Nasr]], [[Khafajah]], and [[Tell Uqair]], and the vast majority of each type have been found at [[Susa]] and Uruk. The tablet blanks, the inscribing method, even the practice of using the reverse for summation, when needed, are the same. They serve the same basic function which is administrative accounting of goods in a centrally controlled society. From that base, there are also differences, the signs themselves being the most obvious but extending to smaller areas such as the order in which the tablet was inscribed, are clear.{{Clarify|reason=What does "are clear" apply to?|date=May 2026}} The many similarities between the numeric systems of Proto-cuneiform and Proto-Elamite have helped in understanding it. Proto-Elamite, in addition to the usual sexagesimal and base-120, also uses a decimal system.<ref>Friberg, Jöran, "Three Thousand Years of Sexagesimal Numbers in Mesopotamian Mathematical Texts", Archive for History of Exact Sciences, vol. 73, no. 2, pp. 183–216, 2019</ref><ref>Friberg, Jöran, "The Early Roots of Babylonian Mathematics: II. Metrological Relations in a Group of Semi-Pictographic Tablets of the Jemdet Nasr Type, Probably from Uruk-Warka", Research Report, 1979-15; University of Göteborg, Department of Mathematics, Chalmers, 1978–79</ref>

[[File:P1180316 Louvre Suse III tablette économique Sb15200 rwk.jpg|thumb|upright=1.2|Economical tablet in Proto-Elamite, Suse III, [[Louvre Museum]], reference Sb 15200, {{circa|3100–2850&nbsp;BC}}]]
[[File:Proto-Elamite tablet with transcription.jpg|thumb|upright=1.2|Proto-Elamite tablet with transcription]]
Beginning around the 9th millennium&nbsp;BC, a token based system came into use in various parts of the ancient Near East. These evolved into marked tokens and later marked envelopes, often called [[Bulla (seal)|clay bullae]].<ref>Schmandt-Besserat, Denise, "The Envelopes That Bear the First Writing", Technology and Culture, vol. 21, no. 3, pp. 357–85, 1980</ref><ref>Schmandt-Besserat, Denise, "Decipherment of the Earliest Tablets", Science, vol. 211, no. 4479, pp. 283–85, 1981</ref><ref>Schmandt-Besserat, Denise. ''Before writing: From counting to cuneiform'', Vol. II, University of Texas Press, 1992</ref><ref>Overmann, Karenleigh A., ''The Material Origin of Numbers: Insights from the Archaeology of the Ancient Near East'', Piscataway, NJ, USA: Gorgias Press, Table 9.2, pp. 169-170, 2019</ref> These are believed to be the basis of Proto-Elamite as well as proto-cuneiform (with many of the tokens, about two-thirds, having been found in Susa). Tokens remained in use after the development of proto-cuneiform and Proto-Elamite.<ref>Denise Schmandt-Besserat, "An Archaic Recording System and the Origin of Writing", Syro Mesopotamian Studies, vol. 1, no. 1, pp. 1–32, 1977</ref><ref>Denise Schmandt-Besserat, "An Archaic Recording System in the Uruk-Jemdet Nasr Period", American Journal of Archaeology, vol. 83, no. 1, pp. 19–48, (Jan. 1979)</ref><ref>Lieberman, Stephen J., "Of Clay Pebbles, Hollow Clay Balls, and Writing: A Sumerian View", American Journal of Archaeology, vol. 84, no. 3, pp. 339–58, 1980</ref><ref>Schmandt-Besserat, D., "Tokens at Susa", Oriens Antiquus 25(1–2), pp. 93–125, 1986</ref>

The earliest tablets contain only lists of numbers. They are found not only at Susa and Uruk, but also at sites without later Proto-Elamite and proto-cuneiform tablets, like [[Tell Brak]], [[Habuba Kabira]], [[Tepe Hissar]], [[Godin Tepe]] and [[Jebel Aruda]].<ref>Schmandt-Besserat, Denise, "The Earliest Precursor of Writing", Scientific American, vol. 238, no. 6, pp. 50–59, 1978</ref><ref>Strommenger, Eva, "The Chronological Division of the Archaic Levels of Uruk-Eanna VI to III/II: Past and Present", American Journal of Archaeology, vol. 84, no. 4, pp. 479–87, 1980</ref><ref>[https://tspace.library.utoronto.ca/bitstream/1807/27127/1/hallo%20report.pdf] Hallo, William W., "Godin Tepe: The Inscriptions", Yale University, 2011</ref><ref>Oates, Joan and Oates, David, "The Reattribution of Middle Uruk Materials at Brak". Leaving No Stones Unturned: Essays on the Ancient Near East and Egypt in Honor of Donald P. Hansen, edited by Erica Ehrenberg, University Park, USA: Penn State University Press, pp. 145–154, 2002</ref><ref>R. Dyson, "The relative and absolute chronology of Hissar H and the proto-Elamite of Northern Iran", In: Chronologie du Prochc Orient/Relative chrono-logics and absolute chronology 16,000–4,000 BC. CNRS International Symposium, Lyon France, 24–28  November, 1986, 13AR Internat. Scr. 379, Oxford, pp. 647–677, 1987</ref>

[[Linear Elamite]] is attested much later, in the last quarter of the {{BCE|3rd millennium}}. It is uncertain whether the Proto-Elamite script was the direct predecessor of [[Linear Elamite]], since it remains largely undeciphered, and a postulated relationship between the two is speculative.

Early on, similarities were noted between Proto-Elamite and the Cretan [[Linear A]] script.<ref>Brice, W., "A Comparison of the Account Tablets of Susa in the Proto-Elamite Script with Those of Hagia Triada in Linear A", KADMOS, 2(1), pp. 27–38, 1963</ref>

==Corpus ==
The Proto-Elamite writing system was used over a large area, from Susa in the west to [[Tepe Yahya]] in the east. The corpus consists of some 1600 tablets, the vast majority unearthed at Susa, where the first two
tablets were found by [[Jacques de Morgan]] in the late 1800s.<ref name="Damerow1989" />

Proto-Elamite tablets have been found at the following sites:
*[[Susa]] (more than 1600 tablets and fragments)<ref>de Mecquenem, R., "Notes Protoélamites", Revue d’Assyriologie et d’archéologie Orientale, vol. 50, no. 4, pp. 200–04, 1956</ref><ref>Dahl, Jacob L., "Proto-Elamite Tablets and Fragments", Textes cunéiforme du Louvre 32, Khéops / Louvre Editions Publishing, Paris, 2019</ref>

Glyphs found in Proto-Elamite texts are divided in numerical (with an N prefix) and text (with a M prefix). Of the 1000s of text glyphs in the current corpus, more than half are numerical. The meaning of a numerical glyph may depend on which system it is being used in, decimal (D), sexagesimal (S), bisexagesimal (B), or capacity (C).<ref>[https://aclanthology.org/2023.cawl-1.9.pdf]Born, Logan, et al., "Disambiguating Numeral Sequences to Decipher Ancient Accounting Corpora", Proceedings of the Workshop on Computation and Written Language (CAWL 2023), 2023</ref><ref> Alimirzoev, A. N. and Yu. B. Yusifov, "On the decipherment of numerical signs of the Proto-Elamite script", Vestnik drevnej istorii, pp. 3-20, 1993</ref><ref>[https://nbsh.basu.ac.ir/jufile?issue_pdf=760#page=91]Yousefi, R., Afshari Salaki, H. & Etemadifar, D., "The Scale of Accounting: An Examination of High-Volume Numerical Records in Proto-Elamite
Tablets from Susa hosted in Iran National Museum", Archaeological Research of Iran, 15(45), pp. 91-113, 2025</ref>

A significant number of Proto-Elamite tablets remain unpublished.<ref>Jacob L. Dahl, "Oxford Proto-Elamite", De Babylone aux routes d’Arabie, portrait d’une orientaliste: Hommage à Béatrice André-Salvini, pp. 85-88, 2025</ref>

== Decipherment attempts ==
[[File:Tablilla del periodo protoelamita - MARQ.jpg|thumb|Proto-Elamite tablet found at Tepe Yahya]]
The first step in deciphering an unknown writing system is publishing the corpus and developing a proposed sign list.<ref>Gelb, I. J., and R. M. Whiting, "Methods of Decipherment", Journal of the Royal Asiatic Society of Great Britain and Ireland, no. 2, pp. 95–104, 1975</ref> The texts have now been mostly published but the sign list is still in progress. Proto-Elamite has many singleton signs (like early stages of proto-cuneiform) because texts are primarily consumed locally. There is disagreement over whether some signs are different or merely variants; but by 1974 enough of a consensus on the signs was reached that decipherment could advance.
<ref>Piero Meriggi, "La scritura proto-elamica. Parte Ia: La scritura e il contenuto dei testi", Rome, 1971</ref><ref>Piero Meriggi,"La scritura proto-elamica. Parte IIa: Catalogo dei segni", Rome, 1974</ref><ref>Piero Meriggi, "La scritura proto-elamica. Parte IIIa: Testi", Rome, 1974</ref><ref>Dahl, Jacob L., "The proto-Elamite writing system", in The Elamite World, pp. 383–396, 2018</ref>

In 2012, Dr Jacob Dahl of the [[University of Oxford]] announced a project to publish high-quality images of Proto-Elamite clay tablets online. He hopes that [[crowdsourcing]]  by academics and amateurs working together will make progress.<ref>[https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/business-19964786 Breakthrough in world's oldest undeciphered writing – Sean Coughlan – 2012-10-25 BBC]</ref> Nearly 1600 Proto-Elamite tablets are now on line.<ref>[http://cdli.ucla.edu Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative]. [[University of California, Los Angeles]]. Retrieved 9 March 2015.</ref> Materials were put online<ref name="bbc_crowd">{{cite news |last1=Coughlan |first1=Sean |title='Crowd-sourcing' website to decipher ancient writing |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/education-20066885 |work=BBC News |date=24 October 2012}}</ref> on a wiki of the Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative.<ref>{{cite web |title=Proto-Elamite|publisher=Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative Wiki |url=https://cdli.ox.ac.uk/wiki/proto-elamite |website=cdli.ox.ac.uk |language=en}}</ref>

In 2020, {{ill|François Desset|fr}}, of the Laboratoire Archéorient in Lyon, announced a proposed decipherment and translation.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.sciencesetavenir.fr/archeo-paleo/archeologie/breaking-the-code-en-craquant-une-ecriture-non-dechiffree-vieille-de-plus-de-4000-ans-un-francais-remet-en-cause-la-seule-invention-de-l-ecriture-en-mesopotamie_149795|title = Un Français déchiffre une écriture de plus de 4000 ans|date = 7 December 2020}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.tehrantimes.com/news/455638/Iranian-plateau-gave-birth-to-writing-French-archaeologist|title = Iranian plateau gave birth to writing: French archaeologist|date = 11 December 2020}}</ref> In 2022 he proposed sign forms for Proto-Elamite (recasting it as "Early Proto-Iranian").<ref>Desset, François, Tabibzadeh, Kambiz, Kervran, Matthieu, Basello, Gian Pietro and Marchesi, and Gianni, "The Decipherment of Linear Elamite Writing", Zeitschrift für Assyriologie und vorderasiatische Archäologie, vol. 112, no. 1, pp. 11–60, 2022</ref><ref>Kelley, Kathryn, et al., "On Newly Proposed Proto-Elamite Sign Values", Iranica Antiqua 57, pp. 1–25, 2022</ref> The proposal was not met with universal agreement.<ref>[https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:51adb1c2-61de-438c-9b29-125addf3d2a1/files/r6w924c626] Dahl, J., "Proto-Elamite and linear elamite, a misunderstood relationship?", Akkadica, 2023</ref>

Although the decipherment of Proto-Elamite remains uncertain, the content of many texts is known. This is because certain signs, notably a majority of the numerical signs, are similar to the neighboring Mesopotamian proto-cuneiform. In addition, a number of the Proto-Elamite signs depict the objects they represent. However, the majority of the Proto-Elamite signs are abstract, and their meanings can only be deciphered through careful [[Graphemics|graphemic]] analysis.<ref>[https://www.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/Preprints/P183.PDF] Robert K. Englund, "The State of Decipherment of Proto-Elamite", in: Stephen Houston, ed. The First Writing: Script Invention as History and Process, Cambridge, UK: Cambridge University Press, pp.&nbsp;100–149, 2004</ref>

An example from a small tablet (Sb 06355) from Susa where most signs are known:<ref>[https://cdli.ucla.edu/P008389]"MDP 17, 191 Artifact Entry", (2001) 2024. Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative (CDLI). July 3, 2024</ref>

While the Elamite language has been suggested as the language of the Proto-Elamite inscriptions, there is no positive evidence of this. The earliest Proto-Elamite inscriptions, being purely [[Ideogram|ideograph]]ic, do not in fact contain any linguistic information nor is it known what language was spoken in the area during the Proto-Elamite Period.<ref>Jöran Friberg, "The Third Millennium Roots of Babylonian Mathematics I-II", Göteborg, 1978/79</ref>
