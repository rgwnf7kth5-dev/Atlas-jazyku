The '''{{lang|la|Appendix Probi}}''' {{gloss|mode=def|[[Marcus Valerius Probus|Probus]]' Appendix}} is the conventional name for a series of five documents believed to have been copied in the seventh or eighth century in [[Bobbio]], Italy.<ref>Quirk 2006</ref> Its name derives from the fact that the documents were found attached to a copy of the {{lang|la|Instituta Artium}}, a treatise named after (but probably not written by) the first-century grammarian [[Marcus Valerius Probus]].<ref>Powell 2007: §1</ref>

The Appendix was likely composed in Rome{{efn|There is a reference on line 134 to {{lang|la|caput africae}}, the name of a grammar school known to have been located in that city.<ref>Barnett 2007: 705</ref>|name=|group=lower-roman}} around the first half of the fourth century AD.<ref>Quirk 2006: 16–21, 300</ref>

It is specifically the third of the five documents that has attracted scholarly attention, as it contains a list of 227 spelling mistakes, along with their corrections, which shed light on the [[Romance linguistics#Phonology|phonological]] and [[Romance linguistics#Other changes|grammatical]] changes that the [[Vulgar Latin|local vernacular]] was experiencing in the early stages of its development into [[Romance languages|Romance]].

The text survives only in a carelessly transcribed water-damaged manuscript of the 7th or 8th century<ref>Rohlfs 1969: 16</ref> which is kept at the {{lang|it|[[Biblioteca Nazionale Vittorio Emanuele III]]|italic=no}}<ref>Quirk 2006</ref> as MS Lat. 1 (formerly Vindobonensis 17).

== Phenomena visible in the spelling mistakes ==
Note that the format is "[correct spelling], not [incorrect spelling]".<ref>Elcock 1960: 28–34</ref> [[Scribal abbreviation]]s have been expanded.
