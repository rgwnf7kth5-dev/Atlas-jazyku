This is a list of words coming to [[English language|English]] from or via [[Czech language|Czech]], or originating in the [[Lands of the Bohemian Crown]], often called [[Czech lands]]. Words and expressions derived from the Czech language are called [[Bohemisms]].

*'''[[Absurdistan]]''' (in Czech ''Absurdistán'') – word created by [[Eastern Bloc]] dissidents, passed into English mainly through works of [[Václav Havel]]. 
*'''Dollar''' – from Czech '''tolar''' (taler), a silver coin used in the [[Czech lands]] since the 15th century.
*'''[[háček]]''' – a [[diacritical mark]], literally "little hook", e.g. '''č''' is letter '''c''' having háček. Also known as "caron".
*'''[[howitzer]]''' – from ''houfnice'', a 15th-century [[Hussite]] catapult; ''houf'' meaning ''crowd'' or ''band''
*'''[[kolache]]''' – from ''koláč'' or ''koláček''.
*'''koczwarism''' – [[Erotic asphyxiation|Sexual asphyxiophilia]] in medical slang; after [[Frantisek Kotzwara|František Kočvara]]
*'''[[polka]]''' – from ''Polák'' or ''polský'', a Czech dance named in remembrance of the [[November Uprising]] of 1830; or from ''Půlka'', in English ''half'' because of its tempo
*'''[[Pram (ship)|pram]]''' – from Czech ''prám'', a flatbottomed boat, through Dutch praam and Middle Dutch praem <ref>{{Cite web | url=http://www.yourdictionary.com/pram | title=Pram Definitions &#124; What does pram mean? &#124; Best 10 Definitions of Pram }}</ref>
*'''[[robot]]''' – from Czech ''robot'' (machine resembling a human being), introduced in [[Karel Čapek#Etymology of robot|Karel Čapek's]] play [[R.U.R. (Rossum's Universal Robots)|R.U.R.]] from the 1920s.
*'''[[Semtex]]''' – a plastic explosive named after Semtín, part of the city of [[Pardubice]], Czech Republic, location of its manufacturer.
*'''Slivovice''' ([[Slivovitz]]), plum brandy – from Czech word slíva (plum)

Sometimes it is mistakenly claimed '''[[Hocus Pocus (magic)|hocus-pocus]]''' has Czech origin since ''pokus'' means ''trial'' (attempt) or [[experiment]].
